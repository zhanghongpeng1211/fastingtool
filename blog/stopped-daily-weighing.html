Why I Stopped Weighing Myself Every Day (And Started Tracking Trends Instead)

S
Sarah Patel · Graphic Designer · Austin, TX
📅 2024-06-12 ⏱️ 5 min
Advertisement Ad space reserved

I used to weigh myself every morning. First thing. Before coffee, before water, after bathroom. Same scale, same spot on the floor. I thought I was being "data-driven."

I was actually being neurotic.

The problem with daily weighing is that weight fluctuates. A lot. I would see a 0.5 kg drop and feel amazing. Then a 0.3 kg gain the next day and feel like a failure. Even though I knew it was just water, or salt, or hormones, or whatever — the emotional reaction was real.

After 6 weeks of this emotional rollercoaster, I made a rule: weigh myself once a week, same day, same time. Sunday morning, 9 AM, post-bathroom, pre-breakfast. That's it.

And I started tracking the weekly trend instead of daily noise. The calculator's trend tab actually helped with this — it averages things out and shows you the direction, not the daily chaos.

Here's what I learned: my weight drops in steps, not slopes. I'll be flat for 5-7 days, then drop 0.8 kg overnight. Then flat again. Then another drop. If I was weighing daily, those flat periods would have driven me insane. But seeing the weekly trend made it obvious: the overall direction was down.

I also started measuring my waist. Every two weeks. Because sometimes the scale doesn't move but your clothes fit better. That's real progress the scale can't capture.

The calculator now has a "trend tracking" feature where you can log weight, fasting hours, and mood. I use it to see the correlation between my fasting consistency and my weight trend. Spoiler: they're related, but not perfectly. Some weeks I fast great and lose nothing. Some weeks I slip up and drop weight. Bodies are weird.

My current approach: weekly weigh-in, bi-weekly measurements, daily fasting hour tracking. The daily stuff is about behavior. The weekly stuff is about results. Keeping them separate keeps me sane.

If you're obsessed with your scale, try hiding it for a week. Just track your fasting hours and how you feel. See what happens. You might be surprised by how little the number matters.

— Sarah
